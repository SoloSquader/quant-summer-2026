## Notation Learned

∀ means “for all.”

∃ means “there exists.”

¬ means “not.”

ℤ means the set of integers.

## Negation Rules Practiced

Negating “for all”:

¬(∀x, P(x)) = ∃x such that ¬P(x)

Negating “there exists”:

¬(∃x, P(x)) = ∀x, ¬P(x)

Negating an if-then statement:

¬(P → Q) = P and ¬Q

## Problems Completed

### Problem 1

Translated the statement into symbols:

“For every integer n, n² is greater than or equal to 0.”

### Problem 2

Translated the symbolic statement into words:

∃ n ∈ ℤ such that n² = 16

### Problem 3

Disproved a universal statement with a counterexample:

“For every integer n, if n is odd, then n² is even.”

### Problem 4

Negated a universal statement and used a counterexample to show the original statement was false.

Original statement:

For every integer n, n² is even.

### Problem 5

Compared two quantified statements:

Statement A:
For every integer n, there exists an integer m such that m > n.

Statement B:
There exists an integer m such that for every integer n, m > n.

Result:
Statement A is true.
Statement B is false.

Main concept:
∀n ∃m is not the same as ∃m ∀n.
